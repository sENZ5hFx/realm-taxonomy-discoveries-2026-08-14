# Related Work and Distinctions

This document maps the three novelties to the closest existing frameworks and states precisely how each differs. This is not a literature review — it is a distinction map. Its purpose is to help researchers locate these novelties within the existing landscape and verify that the claims do not reduce to prior work.

---

## Causal Information Horizons

### Closest existing frameworks

**Pearl's do-calculus and the identification problem** (Pearl, 2000; Tian & Pearl, 2002)  
Addresses whether a causal effect can be estimated from observational data given a known (but possibly incomplete) causal graph. The identification problem is an estimation problem under assumed graph structure.

*Distinction:* Causal Information Horizons addresses whether the graph itself can be fully known by an observer embedded within the system being modeled. The question is not "can we estimate X given this graph" but "can an embedded observer ever complete the graph." The horizon is a structural limit on graph knowledge, not an estimation limit under a known graph.

**Godel incompleteness (applied to causal systems)**  
Some treatments of causal reasoning draw analogies to Godel incompleteness — that sufficiently expressive causal systems contain truths unprovable within the system.

*Distinction:* Godel incompleteness is a property of formal systems. Causal Information Horizons is a claim about embedded observers in physical causal systems — specifically that an observer cannot have complete causal knowledge of a system that contains the observer. The mechanism is different (observer embedding vs. formal expressiveness) and the domain is different (physical causality vs. formal logic).

**Quantum observer effects**  
Observer-dependent phenomena in quantum mechanics establish that measurement affects the system being measured.

*Distinction:* Causal Information Horizons does not require quantum mechanics. The observer-embedding problem exists in classical causal systems. The claim is more general.

## Temporal Informational Singularity for Qualia

### Closest existing frameworks

**Integrated Information Theory (IIT)** (Tononi, 2004; Tononi et al., 2016)  
Proposes that consciousness corresponds to integrated information (phi) — a measure of how much information a system generates as a whole beyond its parts.

*Distinction:* IIT is a complexity measure — more phi, more consciousness. TIS-Qualia is a structural condition — the presence of a specific informational architecture (irreducible temporal self-reference), not a continuous measure. A system could have high phi without reaching a TIS and vice versa. The claims are orthogonal, not competing.

**Global Workspace Theory (GWT)** (Baars, 1988; Dehaene et al., 1998)  
Proposes that consciousness arises from global broadcasting of information across brain areas.

*Distinction:* GWT is a functional/architectural theory about information access. TIS-Qualia is a claim about informational self-reference in the temporal domain. GWT says nothing about the temporal self-reference structure that TIS-Qualia targets.

**Hofstadter's Strange Loops** (Hofstadter, 1979, 2007)  
Proposes that consciousness arises from self-referential loops in symbolic/logical systems.

*Distinction:* Strange loops are logical self-reference. TIS-Qualia specifies temporal self-reference — the condition where a system's model of its present state cannot be described without reference to the experience of having that state. The temporal dimension is the structurally novel element.

**Higher-Order Theories (HOT)** (Rosenthal, 1997)  
Proposes that conscious states are states one is aware of having — mental states with higher-order representations.

*Distinction:* HOT is a representational theory about the content of conscious states. TIS-Qualia is an informational-structural theory about the temporal architecture of self-reference. HOT asks "what makes a state conscious"; TIS-Qualia asks "what informational structure is necessary for qualia to occur."

## Informational as Active Cascade Medium

### Closest existing frameworks

**Shannon information theory** (Shannon, 1948)  
Defines information as reduction of uncertainty, explicitly substrate-neutral. Makes no claims about information shaping causal flow.

*Distinction:* Shannon's framework treats information as a measure, not a medium. Informational as Active Cascade Medium claims that informational structure actively channels causal possibility space — a claim Shannon's framework neither makes nor rules out, but does not address.

**Wolfram's computational universe** (Wolfram, 2002, 2020)  
Proposes that the physical universe is fundamentally computational — that physical reality is the output of simple computational rules.

*Distinction:* Wolfram's claim is metaphysical (reality *is* computation). Informational as Active Cascade Medium is a design claim: informational architecture functions as a causal intervention in systems where information structures are dense enough to channel possibility space. The claim is empirically tractable (genomes, trained networks) and does not require a commitment to computational metaphysics.

**Wheeler's "It from Bit"** (Wheeler, 1990)  
Proposes that physical reality is fundamentally informational — that every physical quantity derives from binary choices.

*Distinction:* Wheeler's claim is cosmological. Informational as Active Cascade Medium is a systems-design claim about observable, constructed informational structures. The scope is different.

**Causal emergence** (Hoel et al., 2013)  
Proposes that macro-scale causal descriptions can be more effective than micro-scale ones, establishing that causation operates at multiple scales.

*Distinction:* Causal emergence is about the effectiveness of causal descriptions at different scales. Informational as Active Cascade Medium is about information structures as active shapers of causal cascades. The claims are complementary — causal emergence establishes that macro-scale causal descriptions are real; Informational as Active Cascade Medium proposes the mechanism by which high-level informational structures exert that causal influence.
